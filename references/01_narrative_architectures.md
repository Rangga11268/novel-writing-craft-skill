# Narrative Architectures & Plot Frameworks

Comprehensive reference for selecting, calibrating, and executing story structures across long-form fiction.

---

## 1. The Classic Three-Act Paradigm (Syd Field, Robert McKee, K.M. Weiland)

A balance of 25% Act I, 50% Act II, and 25% Act III.

### Act I: Setup (0% – 25%)
* **0% - 1%: The Hook & Normal World:** Establishes the protagonist in their flawed equilibrium. Reveals their core vulnerability and "The Lie" they believe.
* **10% - 12%: Inciting Incident (Catalyst):** An external disruption that shatters the status quo. The protagonist cannot simply ignore this event.
* **12% - 20%: Debate / Resistance:** The protagonist attempts to solve the problem using old habits, denial, or temporary fixes.
* **20% - 25%: Plot Point 1 (Door of No Return):** A proactive choice or irreversible crisis forces the protagonist into a new world/situation where turning back is impossible.

### Act II: Confrontation & Rising Action (25% – 75%)
* **25% - 37%: Exploration & Reactive Phase:** The protagonist navigates unfamiliar rules, gathers allies, and reacts to obstacles.
* **37%: First Pinch Point:** Direct reminder of the antagonist's true power. Stakes are raised; consequences of failure become visible.
* **50%: The Midpoint (Crucial Shift):**
  * Transition from **reactive** to **proactive**.
  * Often features a *False Victory* (premature triumph that sows the seeds of disaster) or a *False Defeat* (apparent total failure forcing renewed determination).
  * The protagonist gains key insight into the true nature of the conflict.
* **50% - 62%: Bad Guys Close In & Proactive Phase:** The protagonist takes the initiative, but opposition intensifies internally and externally.
* **62%: Second Pinch Point:** The antagonist strikes a devastating blow. The safety net disappears.
* **75%: Plot Point 2 / All Hope Is Lost (The Nadir):** The protagonist's primary plan shatters completely. Mentor lost, secret exposed, or catastrophic defeat.

### Act III: Resolution & Transformation (75% – 100%)
* **75% - 80%: Dark Night of the Soul:** Emotional processing of loss. The protagonist abandons "The Want" and embraces "The Need/Truth."
* **80% - 90%: The Climax:** Final confrontation. Both the external conflict (the plot) and the internal conflict (character wound) are resolved simultaneously.
* **90% - 100%: Resolution & Dénouement:** The new normal. Subplots resolved, emotional catharsis released, and permanent transformation confirmed.

## 2. Save the Cat! Writes a Novel (Blake Snyder & Jessica Brody)

15-beat timing breakdown for commercial pacing:

| Beat | Percentage | Purpose |
|---|---|---|
| **Opening Image** | 0% – 1% | Visual snapshot of the "before" state. |
| **Theme Stated** | 5% | Incidental line of dialogue expressing the thematic truth. |
| **Setup** | 1% – 10% | The flawed world and "Six Things That Need Fixing". |
| **Catalyst** | 10% | Life-altering incident. |
| **Debate** | 10% – 20% | Reluctance, resistance, and calculating risks. |
| **Break into Two** | 20% | Active decision to enter Act II. |
| **B Story** | 22% | Introduction of the core relationship carrying the theme. |
| **Fun and Games** | 20% – 50% | Delivery of the premise promise (*trailer moments*). |
| **Midpoint** | 50% | Stakes raised; False Victory or False Defeat. |
| **Bad Guys Close In**| 50% – 75% | Doubts, cracks in relationships, external enemy closes net. |
| **All Hope Is Lost** | 75% | Whiff of death; rock bottom. |
| **Dark Night of Soul**| 75% – 80% | Grieving the old self. |
| **Break into Three** | 80% | The "Aha!" epiphany integrating A & B story lessons. |
| **Finale** | 80% – 99% | 5-step execution: Reassemble $\rightarrow$ Plan $\rightarrow$ High Tower Surprise $\rightarrow$ Dig Deep $\rightarrow$ New Plan. |
| **Final Image** | 99% – 100% | Mirror image of Opening Image proving irreversible growth. |

## 3. The 7-Point Story Structure (Dan Wells)

Optimal for symmetric planning from ending to beginning:

1. **Hook:** Starting point, diametrically opposed to the Resolution.
2. **Plot Turn 1:** Call to adventure; moves character from status quo.
3. **Pinch Point 1:** Antagonist pressure applied; reveals stakes.
4. **Midpoint:** Character transitions from defensive reaction to offensive action.
5. **Pinch Point 2:** Worst-case scenario strikes; forces total vulnerability.
6. **Plot Turn 2:** Discovers the missing piece or makes the critical sacrifice.
7. **Resolution:** Climax and triumphant transformation.

## 4. The Story Grid: 5 Commandments of Storytelling (Shawn Coyne)

Every narrative unit (beat, scene, sequence, act, global story) must satisfy:

1. **Inciting Incident:** Causal or coincidental event that disrupts life balance.
2. **Progressive Complications:** Escalating friction culminating in a **Turning Point** (Action or Revelation).
3. **Crisis:** The unavoidable dilemma:
   * *Best Bad Choice:* Choosing between two terrible options.
   * *Irreconcilable Goods:* Choosing between two virtues that cannot coexist.
4. **Climax:** The character's active, irreversible decision in action.
5. **Resolution:** The immediate fallout and new life value status.
