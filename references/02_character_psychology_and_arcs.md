# 3D Character Psychology & Arc Trajectories

A guide to engineering emotionally resonant, non-flat characters rooted in developmental psychology.

---

## 1. The Core Character Triad

Characters do not exist as static lists of personality traits. They exist as dynamic coping mechanisms reacting to past trauma.

```
       [ THE GHOST / WOUND ]
(Origin event causing deep pain or fear)
                 │
                 ▼
      [ THE LIE THE CHARACTER BELIEVES ]
(Flawed defense mechanism to prevent future pain)
                 │
         ┌───────┴───────┐
         ▼               ▼
   [ THE WANT ]     [ THE NEED ]
 (External goal)   (Internal truth)
 (Preserves Lie)   (Shatters Lie)
```

### Definitions:
* **The Ghost / Wound:** A specific, concrete backstory event (loss, betrayal, abandonment, poverty, failure) that left an emotional scar.
* **The Lie:** The distorted worldview the character formed to survive. E.g.:
  * *"If I remain powerless, I will be crushed. Only ruthless power keeps me safe."*
  * *"If I love someone, they will become my weakness and die."*
  * *"I am only valuable when I produce flawless intellectual results."*
* **The Want:** The concrete, tangible goal the character pursues in Act I and II. They mistakenly believe achieving this will heal them, but it only reinforces The Lie.
* **The Need:** The spiritual, emotional, or moral truth the character must embrace to become whole, even if it requires surrendering The Want.

---

## 2. The Four Archetypal Character Arcs

### A. Positive Change Arc (The Hero's Growth)
* **Starting State:** Believes The Lie, resists The Truth, pursues The Want.
* **Crucible:** Act II forces them into situations where The Lie repeatedly fails them.
* **The Epiphany (All Hope Is Lost / Dark Night):** They recognize The Lie's toxicity and consciously abandon The Want.
* **Climax:** They embrace The Need/Truth and win the climax through self-actualization.
* *Examples:* Elizabeth Bennet (*Pride and Prejudice*), Zuko (*Avatar: The Last Airbender*).

### B. Flat Arc (The Testing Agent / Moral Compass)
* **Starting State:** Already knows The Truth from the beginning.
* **Crucible:** The surrounding world or society is broken, corrupt, or in denial.
* **Function:** The character is tested relentlessly to abandon The Truth, but holds firm.
* **Impact:** They do not transform; instead, they transform the characters and environment around them.
* *Examples:* Sherlock Holmes, Katniss Everdeen (regarding human dignity), Paddington Bear.

### C. Disillusionment Arc (The Loss of Innocence)
* **Starting State:** Holds an idealistic, naive view of the world.
* **Crucible:** Confronted by the grim, cynical reality of life, institutional corruption, or personal betrayal.
* **End State:** Overcomes their naive Lie, but the resulting Truth is tragic, cold, and hollow.
* *Examples:* Nick Carraway (*The Great Gatsby*), Winston Smith (*1984*).

### D. Corruption Arc (The Tragedy / The Fall)
* **Starting State:** Possesses moral potential, but harbors an unhealed Wound.
* **Crucible:** Presented with shortcuts to power, status, or protection through corrupt means.
* **The Choice:** At the Midpoint and Crisis, they repeatedly choose The Want over moral integrity.
* **End State:** Complete moral decay. They achieve external dominion or destruction at the cost of their soul.
* *Examples:* Michael Corleone (*The Godfather*), Walter White (*Breaking Bad*), Macbeth.

## 3. Character Voice & Distinct Linguistic Fingerprints

To prevent all characters from sounding like the author or a generic AI:

1. **Vocabulary Density & Domain:**
   * An engineer/programmer thinks in causal logic, data validation, syntax, and inputs/outputs.
   * An aristocrat/politician speaks in implied favors, masked directives, and passive-aggressive etiquette.
   * A street-smart survivor uses short declarative statements, hyper-awareness of physical exits, and transactional dialogue.
2. **Sentence Rhythm & Pacing:**
   * Neurotic/anxious characters: fragmented sentences, mid-thought corrections, interruptions.
   * Dominant/powerful characters: calm cadence, long silences, declarative statements, rare justifications.
3. **Conversational Armor:**
   * What does this character use to deflect intimacy? Humor? Sarcasm? Pedantic corrections? Total silence?
