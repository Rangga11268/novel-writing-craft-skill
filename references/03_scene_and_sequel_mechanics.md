# Scene & Sequel Mechanics: The Engine of Narrative Momentum

Based on the foundational work of Dwight V. Swain (*Techniques of the Selling Writer*) and Jack M. Bickham.

---

## 1. The Two Alternating Rhythms of Storytelling

A well-paced novel is an alternating chain of **action** and **reaction**:

```
[ SCENE (Action/Drive) ] ──► [ SEQUEL (Processing/Pivot) ] ──► [ SCENE ] ──► ...
Goal ──► Conflict ──► Disaster   Reaction ──► Dilemma ──► Decision   Goal ──► ...
```

---

## 2. Anatomy of a SCENE (Goal-Oriented Action)

The function of a Scene is to move the external plot forward through active struggle.

### Component 1: Goal (Must be concrete, immediate, measurable)
* The POV character enters the scene with a specific, conscious objective.
* *Weak Goal:* "He wanted to feel better." (Vague, internal).
* *Strong Goal:* "He needed to copy the encrypted database files onto a flash drive before the security guard completed his 10-minute patrol." (Specific, timed, observable).

### Component 2: Conflict (Dynamic resistance)
* The character encounters active resistance while pursuing the Goal.
* Sources of conflict:
  * Another character with an opposing goal.
  * Environmental/physical barriers.
  * Internal psychological limitations under pressure.

### Component 3: Disaster (The Turning Point / K.O.)
* The scene cannot end in an easy, unqualified victory (which kills narrative tension).
* Swain’s 3 Disaster Outcomes:
  1. **"No!"** (Total failure to achieve the Goal).
  2. **"Yes, BUT..."** (The Goal is achieved, but a new, worse complication is immediately introduced).
  3. **"No, AND FURTHERMORE..."** (Not only did the Goal fail, but an unexpected catastrophe occurred).

---

## 3. Anatomy of a SEQUEL (Emotional Processing & Transition)

The function of a Sequel is to process the emotional fallout of the Disaster and generate a new Goal.

### Component 1: Reaction (Visceral & Emotional Shock)
* The character absorbs the disaster.
* Somatic, physical responses: adrenaline crash, tears, nausea, rage, numbness.
* Show the raw, unfiltered emotional state immediately after the blow.

### Component 2: Dilemma (Cognitive Analysis)
* The character realizes the old plan is dead. They review their remaining options.
* Must present a real predicament:
  * Option A is dangerous.
  * Option B is dishonorable.
  * Option C seems impossible.
* The character wrestles with the stakes, evaluating trade-offs.

### Component 3: Decision (The Pivot)
* The character makes a choice among the difficult options.
* The decision must be proactive.
* **Crucial Rule:** The moment the decision is made, it immediately becomes the **GOAL** of the very next SCENE.

---

## 4. Calibrating Scene vs. Sequel Proportions

* **Fast-Paced Action / Thriller:** Scenes are long and intense; Sequels are compressed into a few paragraphs or single lines ("He took two seconds to wipe the blood from his brow, picked up the keycard, and headed for the roof").
* **Character-Driven Drama / Romance:** Sequels expand to allow profound psychological introspection, dialogue processing, and internal relationship re-evaluations.
