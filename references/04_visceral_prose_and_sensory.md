# Visceral Prose & Sensory Execution: Show, Don't Tell Mastery

A comprehensive diagnostic manual for translating abstract thoughts into immersive, visceral prose.

---

## 1. The Three Layers of Visceral Showing

When communicating character emotion, never tell the reader the label of the emotion (*"He was terrified"*, *"She felt sad"*). Instead, engage the three visceral layers:

### Layer 1: Somatic & Autonomic Physiological Responses (Involuntary)
The body reacts before the conscious brain can lie:
* **Fear / Panic:** Peripheral vasoconstriction (cold fingertips, clammy skin), rapid shallow thoracic breathing, dryness in the back of the throat, adrenaline tremors, tunnel vision.
* **Anger / Hostility:** Mandibular clenching (teeth clicking/aching), surge of blood to the temples, burning sensation across the neck, dilation of nostrils.
* **Shame / Vulnerability:** Skin flushing hot across the sternum and ears, inability to meet direct gaze, involuntary slouching of the cervical spine.
* **Desire / Attraction:** Sensitization of the skin, heightened awareness of the other person's proximity, altered heart rate, dilation of pupils.

### Layer 2: Behavioral Tells & Micro-Actions (Semi-conscious)
Unconscious physical habits that betray internal conflict:
* Checking a non-existent watch or phone screen repeatedly to avoid eye contact.
* Smoothing down a collar or pressing thumbs into fingernails until the beds turn white.
* Stepping slightly back to maintain an unobstructed line of sight to the door.

### Layer 3: Objective Correlative (T.S. Eliot)
Projecting internal psychology onto external sensory details and setting:
* An emotionally detached character notices the cold geometry of fluorescent light bulbs humming at 60 Hz.
* A grief-stricken character fixates on a chipped porcelain cup left unwashed in the sink with a dried ring of tea.

## 2. Anti-Telling Lexicon & Transformation Guide

| Abstract Telling (Banned) | Visceral Showing (Industry Standard) |
|---|---|
| *"He was terrified of the dark alley."* | *"The brick walls pinched the alleyway down to a slit of gray sky. His sneakers scuffed against wet cardboard, each squeak echoing against iron fire escapes like a dropped hammer."* |
| *"She felt humiliated in front of her peers."* | *"Heat climbed her collarbones like boiling water. Her gaze fixed on the frayed hem of her own sneakers while the muffled laughter in the lecture hall turned into white noise in her ears."* |
| *"The old man was lonely."* | *"The kitchen table had four chairs, but three were pushed under so tightly their wooden legs had left permanent indents in the linoleum. Only the single ceramic bowl beside the salt shaker was ever moved."* |
| *"He was deeply in love with him."* | *"Whenever Julian spoke, Adrian’s hand paused over his keyboard. He didn’t interrupt, didn’t look at his phone. He just watched the way Julian’s lips formed around mathematical terms, breathing in the faint scent of rain dampening Julian’s oversized coat."* |

## 3. Five-Sense Calibration Protocol

Every chapter must engage at least **three different senses**:
1. **Visual:** Contrast, lighting, geometric lines, micro-movements.
2. **Auditory:** Frequency, acoustics, hum of appliances, timbre of voice, breath sounds.
3. **Tactile & Thermal:** Temperature, texture, moisture, air currents, friction.
4. **Olfactory:** Chemical aromas, rain on asphalt (petrichor), stale coffee, laundry detergent, fear sweat.
5. **Gustatory:** Bitter taste of adrenaline, metallic tang of blood, dryness of mouth.
