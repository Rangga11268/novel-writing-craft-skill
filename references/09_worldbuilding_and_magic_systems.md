# Worldbuilding & System Design: From Fantasy to High-Tech Realism

Based on Brandon Sanderson’s laws of system design, Ernest Hemingway's Iceberg Principle, and modern speculative worldcraft.

---

## 1. Brandon Sanderson's Three Laws of Magic / Systems

Applicable not just to fantasy magic, but to sci-fi technologies, programming systems, criminal networks, and elite institutional hierarchies:

### Sanderson’s First Law
> *"An author's ability to solve problems with magic/technology in a satisfying way is directly proportional to how well the reader understands said rules."*

* **Hard Systems (Understood rules):** If the reader knows the limitations of Danu's decryption algorithm or a magic spell, using it to escape a trap feels brilliant and earned.
* **Soft Systems (Mysterious/Unexplained):** If the mechanics are vague, using them to solve the climax feels like an unearned cheat (*Deus ex machina*). Soft systems must create wonder or danger, never easy plot solutions.

### Sanderson’s Second Law
> *"Limitations are greater than powers."*

* What a system **cannot** do is exponentially more interesting than what it can do.
* Weaknesses, costs, resource drains, and failure modes generate dramatic conflict.
* *Example:* Danu can crack any firewall, *but* every query sends a ping that reveals his IP address within 90 seconds.

### Sanderson’s Third Law
> *"Expand what you already have before you add something new."*

* Do not invent ten disconnected factions or magical gadgets.
* Take two core elements and extrapolate their cultural, economic, psychological, and legal consequences deeply into society.

## 2. Promise, Progress, Payoff (Brandon Sanderson)

The structural trinity of reader satisfaction:

```
[ PROMISE (Beginning) ] ──► [ PROGRESS (Middle) ] ──► [ PAYOFF (Climax) ]
Contract on genre/stakes       Earned incremental steps      Fulfillment without cheating
```

1. **The Promise:** The opening chapter makes a psychological contract: what kind of ride is this? What are the stakes?
2. **The Progress:** The middle cannot be mere stalling. Every chapter must provide palpable movement—advancing clues, skill progression, shifting alliances, or tightening traps.
3. **The Payoff:** Delivers on the initial promise using the breadcrumbs scattered across the progress phase.

## 3. Hemingway’s Iceberg Principle of Worldbuilding

* **The 10% Above Water:** The vivid, sensory details the characters actually interact with during their daily life (the specific brand of cigarettes, the local dialect slang, the humidity of the subway).
* **The 90% Submerged:** The deep lore, history, geopolitical treaties, and economic charts that the author researched **but never dumps into the prose**.
* **The Test:** The depth of your underwater iceberg creates a palpable aura of gravity and authenticity on the surface without slowing down the story with encyclopedic infodumps.
